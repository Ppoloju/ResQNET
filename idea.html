1The Problem & Our Solution

No jargon. The whole concept in 30 seconds.

📵

The Problem

During disasters, cell towers fail or get overloaded. Every normal "call for help" app stops working — because they all secretly need the internet. Losing network connectivity should never mean losing the ability to seek help.

💡

The Insight

Even with no internet, the phones around you still work. Bluetooth still works. GPS still works. A crowd of 200 people = 200 little radios waiting to help.

🤝

ResQNET

ResQNET lets nearby phones pass your emergency along like a bucket brigade — phone to phone — until it reaches someone who CAN call for help. Powered by on-device AI that works completely offline.

23-Mode Architecture

ResQNET adapts its behavior based on the situation — from everyday readiness to full-scale disaster coordination.

🟢

Normal Mode

Everyday readiness with minimal resource consumption.

  • Background monitoring of sensors (low power)
  • Emergency profile stored securely on device
  • Family circle with contacts & priorities
  • Periodic relay-readiness checks
  • Safety check-ins for peace of mind
🔴

Emergency Mode

Triggered by SOS button or AI detection.

  • SOS activation with 3-2-1 countdown
  • Device-to-device mesh for multi-hop alerts
  • AI classification of emergency type & severity
  • Emergency profile sharing with consent tiers
  • Family notification via priority contacts
  • Live black-box timeline of every action
🟠

Disaster Mode

Activated when multiple emergencies detected in a region.

  • Community mesh expansion — larger ad-hoc network
  • Resource mapping — safe zones, shelters, aid points
  • Crowdsourced awareness — sensor data → disaster map
  • Priority routing — critical alerts get bandwidth first
  • Offline bulletins — "Bridge collapsed", "Shelter open"
  • AI first-aid assistant — evacuation & medical guidance

3What actually happens when you use it

Meet Priya. She is trekking, she falls, and there is zero signal. Here is exactly what she would see — and what happens inside her phone.

1

Priya opens ResQNET and presses the big red SOS button

No account needed. No internet needed. The button is always reachable.

👁 WHAT SHE SEESA big screen counts down 3… 2… 1… with a huge CANCEL button — so a pocket-press can't cause a false alarm.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEThe phone grabs her GPS location (and honestly notes the accuracy), her battery level, and the time. It packs these into a tiny signed "emergency packet" — like a tamper-proof digital ID card for the emergency.
2

The countdown ends → Emergency Mode takes over the screen

👁 WHAT SHE SEESA red status screen with her emergency ID (like IQ-7K2M9X4A), her location, her battery, and a live timeline of everything that happens next.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDENo internet? The packet is saved in an outbox — a queue that keeps trying. ResQNET never gives up just because the network did.
3

Her phone quietly looks for nearby ResQNET phones

👁 WHAT SHE SEES"Nearby devices: searching…" — and every phone found shows up with an anonymous code like RQNET_NODE_A7F3. Never names. Never phone numbers.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEEach nearby ResQNET phone that receives her packet checks it's genuine (signature), stores it, and relays it onward to ITS neighbors — like people passing a message down a line. Duplicate copies are dropped automatically.
4

The message hops closer to help

👁 WHAT SHE SEES"Relay count: 4" — she can watch her emergency physically travel through the crowd, hop by hop, on the timeline.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEEvery hop is counted (max 8) and time-stamped (messages expire after 6 hours) so one old emergency can't circulate forever. Phones with low battery only relay the most critical messages — the network protects its own energy.
5

Someone's phone reaches a "gateway"

👁 WHAT SHE SEES"Responder gateway reached ✓" — the family notification and responder status flip from ⏳ Waiting to ✓ Sent.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEA gateway is any ResQNET device that HAS internet — maybe a rescuer's phone, maybe a villager's. It forwards her emergency to the ResQNET backend, where her family circle and verified responders can see it, along with her emergency profile IF she allowed it (blood group, allergies — her choice, her consent).
6

Multiple emergencies detected → Disaster Mode activates

👁 WHAT SHE SEESThe app switches to an orange Disaster Mode interface showing a community mesh map, nearby shelters, and emergency bulletins from other devices.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEThe mesh expands to form a larger community network. Devices share sensor data to build a crowdsourced disaster map. Critical alerts get priority routing. The AI assistant provides first-aid guidance and evacuation steps.
7

Later — internet returns → everything syncs, nothing duplicates

👁 WHAT SHE SEESThe green "ONLINE" pill returns. Outbox: 0. Timeline complete.
⚙️ WHAT HAPPENS INSIDEThe outbox drains idempotently — a fancy word for "even if it retries 10 times, the server records her emergency exactly once." Her full black-box timeline is preserved for review.

Store & Forward

The phone keeps the message and tries again later. When the link comes back — or a new phone walks into range — the message continues. The emergency doesn't die just because one person walked away.

🔋

Battery-Aware Relay

The network saves its own energy: above 50% battery → relay everything · 20–50% → only HIGH+ priority · below 20% → only CRITICAL. Your SOS always outranks gossip. iQOO's large batteries (6000–7600 mAh) keep the mesh alive longer.

🧠

On-Device AI

On the phone itself — no cloud. It classifies the emergency ("medical", "fire", "lost") and how urgent it is, from a short description or voice text. Powered by iQOO's Snapdragon processor for fast, local inference.

6Inside an emergency packet

Every emergency travels as a tiny signed packet — small enough for a weak radio link, signed so nobody can forge it. Here's what's inside:

🆔 Emergency IDIQ-7K2M9X4A — unique, tracked end-to-end 🕒 Timestampwhen it happened (±5 min anti-replay) 📍 LocationGPS + honest accuracy ("±12 m") or "unavailable" 🔋 Batteryso responders know how long the sender lasts 🩸 Help flags"needs medical" / "needs police" 🧠 AI hintcategory + urgency (advisory only) ⏳ TTL + hop countexpires in 6 h, max 8 hops — no infinite loops ✍️ SignatureHMAC-SHA256 — tamper-proof, per-device key

8Privacy & Security

Emergency data is sensitive. Privacy is a core component, not an afterthought.

  • Your name never travels over the mesh. Devices see anonymous codes, not "Priya".
  • Medical info is yours. Blood group, allergies, medications — shared only if YOU set the visibility (Private / Family / Responders / Nearby helpers) and tick consent.
  • Private by default. Fresh profile = nothing shared with anyone.
  • Camera & microphone are permission-based, clearly indicated, auto-disabled when emergency ends.
  • Passwords are never stored readable — only Argon2id hashes (bank-grade).
  • Every packet is signed. If a malicious phone edits a message, the signature breaks and every other phone rejects it.
  • Medical fields AES-256-GCM encrypted at rest — even a stolen database reveals nothing.
  • Spam can't flood the network — duplicate checks, expiry, hop limits, rate limits.

9What ResQNET can and cannot do

We would rather be trusted than impressive. The honest limits:

✅ CAN

  • Alert every nearby ResQNET phone, even fully offline
  • Relay your emergency hop-by-hop toward people who have signal
  • Reach family + responders the moment any link to the internet exists
  • Classify emergencies and prioritize critical alerts with on-device AI
  • Coordinate community response during large-scale disasters
  • Survive crashes, dead links, reconnections — without duplicates

❌ CANNOT

  • Magic-reach a police station outside radio range
  • Replace calling 100/112/911 when you DO have signal
  • Run background mesh on iOS (Apple blocks it) — full mesh needs native Android
  • Diagnose medical conditions — the AI only classifies urgency

📌 In one sentence: ResQNET augments emergency response — it does not replace official emergency services.
